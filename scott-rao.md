Water activity (a„) and moisture content are different, but they are correlated. Wikipedia defines a as the partial vapor pressure of water in a substance divided by
the standard state partial vapor pressure of water: In layman's terms, a refers to the
amount of “free” (unbound) water in a food relative to its bound water. Microbes
need water for growth; a greater variety of microbes can thrive in food products
with higher a
The range of a in specialty coffee is perhaps 0.45-0.65; high-quality beans
measure about 0.53–0.59. Given this small range and water activity's high correlation with moisture content, it is difficult to isolate the influence of water activity
(a) on the energy required to roast a bean.
Although I can't offer any words of wisdom about how to use a levels to adjust
energy during a roast, I recommend trying to buy green coffee with a in the range
of 0.53-0.59. You may find lovely coffees with a outside of that range, but coffees
in that range tend to taste great and age more gracefully.

Roasting creates water and also drives off the vast majority of the water in
coffee beans.

Weight loss measurements may range from 13%-15% for
a light third-wave roast to greater than 20% for an early-second crack roast, such as
that typical of Starbucks, to almost 25% for unspeakably dark roasts.
